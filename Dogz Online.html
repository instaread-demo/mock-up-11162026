Finding trusted dog breeders in Australia can feel like a daunting task, we hear so much about puppy farms, bad breeding practices and scammers that we often do not know where to start. In this article, we will break it down for you so you can make the best choice in breeders.

We will guide you through the process of finding and working with reputable dog breeders in Australia (and New Zealand), highlighting what to look for, red flags to avoid (and green flags too!), and why Dogz Online is your best resource to find and connect with a trusted Dog Breeder in Australia or NZ.

How to Find and Work with Dog Breeders

Most buyers begin their search online, but general marketplaces like Gumtree, Facebook Marketplace and Trading Post often attract puppy farmers and scammers.

Online Resources

Dogz Online lists verified Dogs Australia and Dogs New Zealand registered breeders of every purebred type, plus current puppies for sale and older dogs for sale. Relying on generic search results is riskier, as fake pages under false names are easy to set up.

Dog Shows and Events

Agricultural, club and agility shows and events let you watch dogs in action and talk with breeders face to face.

Contacting and Communicating with Breeders

Expect a breeder to ask about you, your household and your home, usually through a detailed puppy application.

What Questions to Expect?

Typical questions cover who lives with you, your type of home, children and other pets, time spent at work, spare time for training and exercise, and what you know about the breed. Our guide to 11 questions to expect when applying for a puppy goes further, and you can submit puppy applications through Dogz Online.

What Questions Should You ask?

Ask your own questions too: where the puppies are kept, their age, vaccinations and vet checks, socialisation and exposure to new situations, diet, parent health tests and temperaments, and whether pedigree papers come with the dog.

Setting up Visits

Once approved, meet the dog in person or by video call to check its surroundings and care. Refusing both can signal a scam, a false identity or poor conditions; most breeders happily show you around.

What to Look For in a Dog Breeder

A handful of qualities help point you to the right breeder.

Reputation and Reviews

Read feedback from past buyers and favour breeders with a record of happy customers.

Are they a registered breeder?

Look for Dogs Australia membership, the one pedigree body with international recognition; treat other "pedigree club" claims with care. Dogz Online confirms registration for you.

Are they open and communicative about their Breeding Practices?

Ethical breeders put their dogs' health first, avoid overbreeding and socialise puppies well.

Pedigree Papers

Pedigree papers document breed, health history and lineage, guiding breeders' matings and assuring owners their dog comes from a registered breeder.

Red Flags and Green Flags of Dog Breeders

Red Flags

  1. Lack of Transparency: evasive answers about practices, papers or health checks.
  2. Poor Living Conditions: dirty, crowded or distressed kennels; consider alerting the RSPCA or police.
  3. Overbreeding: litters constantly on offer.

Green Flags

  1. Clean and Healthy Environment: tidy, spacious facilities with happy, active dogs.
  2. Open Communication: detailed, forthcoming information.
  3. Genuine Care for the Dogs: healthy, well-socialised animals.
