From little flutters and ripples to big muscle spasms, BabyCentre mums-to-be reveal what their baby's movements feel like from week to week.

What will my baby's movements feel like?

Movement is usually first felt at around 20 weeks, or from about 16 weeks if you have been pregnant before, and first-time mums may not recognise the fluttering (quickening) straight away. If you have felt nothing by 24 weeks, ask your midwife to check on your baby.

Early bubbling or rolling may later feel slower as space runs out, but movements should stay regular and strong right up to and during labour. If your baby moves less than usual, the movements feel weaker, or they have stopped, call your midwife or maternity unit straight away, and trust your instincts.

12 to 15 weeks

Mums describe faint flutters low down, like tiny bubbles or a flick.

16 to 19 weeks

A soft roll, a ticklish feeling, little pokes or bursting bubbles.

20 to 23 weeks

Pulsing twitches, spasms and a wobbly, jelly-like swimming sensation.

24 to 27 weeks

Stronger turns and punches that push the bump out, though downward kicks can be unpleasant.

28 to 31 weeks

Hard-to-place taps and knocks, plus churning and ripples across the bump.

32 to 35 weeks

Big stretches that reshape the bump, sometimes showing a foot or knee.

36 to 40 weeks

Still strong but slower and heavier, like a tussle or a weighty slide.

Worried about your baby's movements at any stage? Call your midwife or maternity unit straight away, and don't delay getting checked if your baby is moving less than usual, the movements feel weaker, or they have stopped.
