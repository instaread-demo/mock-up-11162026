Paraguay adds criminal association charge against impeached president

ASUNCION, Paraguay: Paraguayan prosecutors have expanded their corruption investigation into the collapse of digital bank ueno, adding a criminal association charge against impeached President Santiago Peña and several senior financial officials.

The bank's collapse earlier this month disrupted nearly three million customer accounts. The crisis led to Peña's removal from office and major changes at the central bank, including the resignation of its head, Carlos Carvallo. The central bank did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Peña and Carvallo, held in pretrial detention on earlier breach-of-trust and personal-gain charges, deny wrongdoing. The new count also names three ex-central bank board members and the regulator's former chief.

Prosecutors allege the group acted to favor ueno and parent firm Grupo Vazquez, with Peña allegedly leading it from the presidency. His lawyer, Ricardo Preda, did not immediately comment.

Peña was a Grupo Vazquez business partner until March 2025. Prosecutors also allege he took illegal payments, including envelopes said to hold US$200,000 in cash.

The document says officials allegedly blocked proper oversight of ueno between June and October 2026 and, prosecutors say, let the insolvent bank keep courting deposits.

Launched in 2021, ueno drew about three million customers before the central bank placed it under resolution on November 3.
